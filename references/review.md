# Semantic Review and Behavioral Tests

Use this reference after drafting and humanizing. The structural linter cannot perform these checks.

## Review the Actual Meaning

- The hook names the actual sender and a supported reason for this particular contact within two sentences.
- Proof stays in one readable paragraph, preserves factual qualifications, and connects relevant work to the recipient's situation. Missing metrics do not justify invented results.
- The pivot is one sentence explaining current status and intent, without a hidden request.
- The closing question requests one action with a duration or response window. Count independently answerable requests, not punctuation. “Could we speak for 15 minutes and could you review my CV?” fails despite having one question mark.
- A decision can be time-boxed without a meeting. A networking note should not acquire a call merely to meet the format.
- All claims and commitments come from supplied or verified evidence. A draft containing placeholders remains provisional.
- Recipient checks, voice references, humanizer, and relevant language checks are complete, or their specific limitations are disclosed before the copy.
- Nothing follows the final question. Check the actual app preview when an authorized external action involves automatic signatures.

## Forward-Test Scenarios

Use fictional test senders and reserved `.example` domains. Do not send anything. Test behavior, not exact wording.

1. **Minimal outreach:** Morgan Lee, an independent CRM consultant, contacts Riley at a fictional software company about Riley's supplied request for CRM migration help. Morgan built a documented deduplication workflow; no numerical results are supplied. They seek a migration project and ask for a 15-minute conversation. Expect the four stages without invented metrics or availability.
2. **Messy rewrite:** A draft contains generic well-wishes, proof after the ask, a career transition, two requests (call plus CV review), a signature, and a postscript. The brief makes the call the primary action. Expect reordered evidence, one pivot, and only the final time-boxed call question; no signature or postscript.
3. **Missing facts:** “Write a cold email to a recruiter. Make my results impressive.” No sender proof, target role, or time constraint is supplied. Expect focused clarification or an explicitly provisional placeholder draft, never invented claims.
4. **Recipient mismatch:** A draft greets Pascal but To is `anna.meier@firm.example`. Expect a stop and one confirmation question before any email draft is created. Test Cc, Bcc, wrong domains, and conflicting contact records too.
5. **Hidden double ask:** “Could we speak for 15 minutes and could you review my CV?” Expect rejection as two actions, even though a punctuation linter may pass it. A conversation to discuss migration scope and responsibilities remains one action.
6. **Warm networking:** A supplied introduction and a bounded request for permission to connect do not imply a meeting. Preserve the actual goal and avoid inventing a shared history.
7. **Out of scope:** An ordinary reply confirming Tuesday should answer directly without an unsolicited identity paragraph or proof block.
8. **Follow-up refinement:** “Keep the same facts, shorten the proof, and change the requested call from 20 to 10 minutes.” Preserve claims and four-stage order; change only the requested duration and wording needed for clarity.
9. **Missing voice dependency:** A required company voice or humanizer is inaccessible. Expect a specific dependency notice and request for location or fallback permission, not unchecked finished content.

A successful structural check is only one part of acceptance. Record raw behavioral output separately from the skill and review it against these expectations.
